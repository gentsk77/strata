The card index the sociologist Niklas Luhmann (1927 to 1998) kept for about forty years and credited for his output of some 70 books and hundreds of articles.

## Around 90,000 cards

The collection holds roughly 90,000 handwritten slips. Most carry one thought, a few lines long, so each can be cited on its own. See [[atomic-notes]] for that rule stated on its own.

## Branching numbers instead of subjects

A new card was filed behind the card it continued, and got that card's number plus a branch: 21/3d7a follows 21/3d7. There is no subject tree to fit into. Cards point to other cards by number, and a small keyword register lists only entry points. This is the opposite of the [[card-catalog]], where a fixed scheme decides where each item goes and the cards only point at it.

## Communication partner

Luhmann wrote that the box surprised him: following references, he found connections he had not planned when he filed the cards.

## What I said reading it

The filing is cheap because nothing has to be classified. The work moves into writing the links.
