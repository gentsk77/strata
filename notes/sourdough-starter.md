What a sourdough starter is, and why bakers keep the same one going for years.

## Two kinds of microbe

A starter is flour and water left to ferment. It ends up holding wild yeasts, which make carbon dioxide and alcohol, and lactic acid bacteria, which make lactic and acetic acid. The bacteria usually outnumber the yeasts by roughly a hundred to one.

## Feeding keeps the balance

Each feeding removes part of the culture and adds fresh flour and water. The acid the bacteria make keeps most other microbes out, so a regularly fed starter stays stable for years. Bakers have carried starters across countries and handed them down for generations.
