The sudden expansion of a loaf during its first 10 minutes or so in a hot oven.

## Where the extra rise comes from

Heat speeds the yeast up briefly before killing it at around 60 degrees Celsius, the gases already in the dough expand, and water and alcohol turn to vapour. A loaf can grow by a third or more. All of this depends on the gas held by the dough described in [[why-sourdough-rises]].

## What stops it

The rise ends when the outside sets into crust. Bakers add steam early to keep the surface moist and stretchy for longer, which also delays the browning of the [[maillard-reaction]] until the loaf has finished growing.

## What I said reading it

Steam buys time for the rise and spends it from the crust.
