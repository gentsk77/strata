The personal notebooks in which European readers from the Renaissance on copied passages they wanted to keep, sorted under headings such as Love, Death or Friendship.

## Locke's index

Locke published a method for indexing such a book: two index pages, with a cell for each first letter and following vowel of a heading. A new heading went on the next free page and its page number into the matching cell, so the book never needed planned space per subject.

## Headings, not links

Every passage still sat under one heading. Finding it from another subject depended on the reader remembering where it went, which is the limit the [[zettelkasten]] removed by linking cards to each other.

## What I said reading it

Locke solved where to put the next page, not how to reach a passage from a second subject.
