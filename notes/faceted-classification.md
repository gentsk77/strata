A library classification published in India in 1933 that describes a subject by combining a few independent facets instead of picking one slot in a fixed tree.

## Five fundamental facets

Ranganathan named five facets, abbreviated PMEST: Personality (the core thing), Matter (material), Energy (process or action), Space (place) and Time. A book on the history of baking in France combines a food, a process, a place and a period. The colon in the name is the punctuation that joins the facets in a class number.

## The point of facets

An enumerative scheme like [[dewey-decimal-classification]] has to list every combined subject in advance. A faceted scheme lists the parts and lets the cataloguer build the combination, so a new combined subject needs no new entry.

## What I said reading it

Facets solve combining at catalogue time, but the book still goes on one shelf.
